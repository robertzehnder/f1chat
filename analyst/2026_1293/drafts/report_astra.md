# Antonelli wins at Monza after a gamble on tyres turns the race his way

Kimi Antonelli started 19th and finished an Italian Grand Prix winner. But the comeback that delivered a Mercedes one-two at Monza hinged on a decision made while the cars were standing still.

During the early red flag, George Russell switched to hard tyres with the intention of reaching the finish. Antonelli chose mediums, committing himself to another stop and trusting that the extra pace would make it worthwhile. For much of the afternoon, Russell's approach looked the stronger bet. Then a virtual safety car gave Antonelli the opening he needed.

He passed his teammate with three laps remaining and pulled clear to win by 3.857 seconds, with Max Verstappen completing the podium. The final move settled the race; the circumstances that made it possible had been taking shape since lap three.

Antonelli had qualified seventh before a 12-place grid penalty sent him to the back end of the field. He wasted little time recovering, gaining seven positions in the opening two laps before the race was suspended following Charles Leclerc's crash.

That left him 12th for the restart and gave Mercedes an opportunity to split its strategies. Russell traded his mediums for hards, while Antonelli swapped his starting set of hards for mediums. Both choices made sense. The question was whether Antonelli could build enough of an advantage to cover the stop Russell was hoping to avoid.

By lap 26, he had worked his way into second and was half a second behind the other Mercedes. With both sets of tyres 22 laps old, Antonelli's mediums were still giving him the edge. Over the next two laps, he whittled the gap down to three tenths.

Getting past was another matter. Russell held the lead, and Antonelli was left close enough to see the opportunity without being able to take it. His recovery had brought him to the front, but he still had a pit stop to make.

The race turned on lap 28.

With Lance Stroll's car having stopped a lap earlier, the virtual safety car was deployed. Antonelli came in for another set of mediums; Russell stayed out. The stop dropped Antonelli to fifth, 13.7 seconds behind his teammate, but it had cost him far less time than a stop under normal racing conditions.

The estimated loss under the VSC was around 13 seconds, compared with roughly 31 seconds at racing speed. That saving gave Antonelli a chance to put his fresh tyres to use before the laps ran out.

Verstappen took the same opportunity, switching to hards and emerging seventh. He would recover to third, while the fight for victory increasingly became a contest between Russell's track position and Antonelli's pace.

By lap 40, Antonelli was 7.1 seconds behind. Eight laps later, the gap was just 0.264 seconds. He had been gaining an average of 0.85 seconds a lap as Russell tried to make his ageing hard tyres last.

Even then, the pass did not come cleanly at the first attempt. Antonelli ran wide at the opening chicane on lap 49, had his lap time deleted for exceeding track limits and fell back to 0.871 seconds behind. After bringing himself within reach, he had briefly let Russell off the hook.

A lap later, he was through.

Russell had led 40 of the race's 53 laps, but once Antonelli moved ahead, he had no answer. Antonelli set the fastest lap on the final tour, a 1m23.504s, and finished nearly four seconds clear.

It was an emphatic finish to a race that had depended on a narrow strategic opening. Without the virtual safety car, Antonelli would have needed to recover roughly another 18 seconds after his stop. His late-race pace suggests he would still have caught Russell rapidly, but catching him before the finish would have been a much bigger ask.

That does not make the result inevitable in either direction. Traffic, tyre wear and the timing of a green-flag stop could all have changed the calculation. What is clear is that the VSC made Antonelli's strategy work: it put him close enough to turn his pace into a winning move.

For Russell, second place was a frustrating return after controlling so much of the afternoon. His hard-tyre strategy had given him the lead and a route to the finish. It became vulnerable when Antonelli was able to stop so cheaply.

Behind the Mercedes pair, Verstappen made the most of the same opportunity to secure third. Pierre Gasly's afternoon went the other way. Having given Alpine its first pole position, he slipped to second by lap two, sixth by lap 10 and seventh at the finish.

Ferrari also left its home race with little to celebrate. Leclerc's early retirement was followed by sixth place for Lewis Hamilton, leaving the Monza crowd to find its celebration in an Italian driver winning for Mercedes.

Antonelli heads to Madrid with 267 points and a 66-point lead over Russell. Monza added another victory to that advantage, and one that demanded more than outright speed. He recovered the positions, made the tyres work and, when the race finally brought Russell within reach, completed the pass.
