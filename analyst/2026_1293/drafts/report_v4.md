# Antonelli's Monza win was set up at the red flag and paid for under the virtual safety car

## The short version

- Antonelli won from 19th, a Mercedes one-two, Russell 3.857s back, Verstappen third.
- He'd qualified seventh. A 12-place grid penalty put him 19th, and the lap-3 red flag had him back up to 12th before anyone had done a racing lap.
- The choice that decided it was made while the cars were parked: Russell took hards to go the distance, Antonelli took mediums.
- A lap-28 virtual safety car priced Antonelli's second stop at 13 seconds instead of roughly 31. Those 18 seconds were the race.
- He closed at 0.85s a lap, went wide at the first chicane on lap 49, and got the job done on lap 50.

## Morning

If you only caught the last three laps on Sunday, you saw the right bit. What you didn't see is why it was ever close. An Italian winning at Monza for the first time since 1966 (that one's from the record books, not our data) makes for a lovely story, and for once the timing sheets tell the same one the crowd did, just with more decimal places.

## The race was decided while it was stopped

Leclerc went off on lap 2, race control called "SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED" and then, a minute later, "RED FLAG - RACE SUSPENDED". While the field sat in the pit lane, 21 cars changed tyres. Russell went medium to hard, the sensible call for a one-stop. Antonelli went the other way, hard to medium.

Nobody thought much of it at the time. It turned out to be the whole afternoon.

## About that grid penalty

Antonelli had qualified seventh; the penalty (an engine change, according to the weekend's reporting, though that's not something the timing data can tell you) cost him 12 places and put him 19th. He'd already got seven of those back in two laps when the red flag came out, so the restart order had him 12th. "From 19th" is true. "From 12th" is what actually happened. Lawson lost eight places to his own penalty, Piastri three.

## Quicker, and stuck

By lap 26 Antonelli was second, 0.5s behind Russell, both on tyres 24 laps old, medium against hard. He got to within three tenths over the next couple of laps and that was that. Monza doesn't let you pass a car of equal speed, and a car half a second quicker isn't enough either, apparently.

Here's the bit the 19th-to-first line hides, and I think it's the whole story: on equal-age rubber the faster car was going nowhere.

## Thirteen seconds instead of thirty-one

Lap 28: "VSC DEPLOYED". Stroll's car had stopped a lap earlier, which is what the reports give as the cause; race control's log doesn't say. Antonelli dived in for a second set of mediums, Russell stayed out on the hards, and Antonelli came back out fifth, 13.7s behind the leader.

How much would that stop have cost at green-flag speed? About 31 seconds, going by the median pit-lane time in this race. Call it an estimate, because it is. The gap between the two numbers is roughly 18 seconds, and 18 seconds is the difference between a fresh set of tyres with a chance and a fresh set with none.

Verstappen did exactly the same thing at exactly the same moment, took a hard, and got the same discount.

## Eight tenths a lap, then a wobble

Lap 40 to lap 48: the gap came down from 7.1s to 0.264s. That's 0.85 seconds a lap, taken off a hard tyre that was 40-odd laps old by then. Then lap 49. He ran wide at the first chicane, and race control put it on the record: "CAR 12 (ANT) TIME 1:28.582 DELETED - TRACK LIMITS AT TURN 1 LAP 49". Four seconds gone, gap back out to 0.871s.

Lap 50, he was through. Three laps to run. He set the fastest lap of the race on the last of them, 1m23.504s, which tells you plenty about how much he had left.

## Take the VSC away

Would he have won anyway? I don't think so. At 0.85 a lap, a 31-second green-flag stop takes about 37 laps to recover and there were 25 left. Russell keeps the lead, Antonelli finishes second in the quickest car on the day, and everyone writes about what might have been.

The pace was real. The cheap stop is what let him spend it. And yes, the 31 seconds is an estimate; I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise.

## Winners and losers

**Winner: Antonelli (1st).** Qualified seventh, started 19th, won. Plenty went his way, and he still had to drive the car that fast for 50 laps with his team-mate in the mirrors for most of them.

**Loser: Russell (2nd).** Led 40 of the 53 laps and lost the race to a timing he had no say in. Should he have pitted under the VSC too? To answer that you'd need to know where he'd have come out, and the data doesn't carry it. Neither, I suspect, did he at the time.

**Winner: Verstappen (3rd).** Same cheap stop as the winner, same drop to seventh, back to third. Same trick, one tyre step down.

**Loser: Gasly (7th).** Alpine's first pole, second by lap 2, sixth by lap 10, seventh at the flag. No incident anywhere in his trace. The car simply drifted back to where its race pace lived.

**Loser: Ferrari (6th + DNF).** Leclerc out on lap 2 after a first-chicane tangle the stewards noted and let go; Hamilton sixth. Whether the tangle caused the crash isn't in the data, and I'm not going to guess.

**Noted: Pérez.** The five-second penalty was for the lap-25 escape-road business at Turn 1, not the start, whatever you heard: "FIA STEWARDS: 5 SECOND TIME PENALTY FOR CAR 11 (PER) - FAILING TO FOLLOW RACE DIRECTORS INSTRUCTIONS – ESCAPE ROAD INSTRUCTIONS". Tsunoda's starting-procedure infringement went to the stewards after the race; the feed has no decision on it.

## Until next week

Antonelli leads Russell by 66 points, 267 to 201. Madrid next, a brand-new circuit with no history in the data at all, which should keep everyone honest. See you Sunday.
