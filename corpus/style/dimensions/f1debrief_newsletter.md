# Style dimensions — F1 Debrief — per-race newsletter analysis

> Distilled 2026-09-07 from 6 dev-split documents (see corpus/manifests/distill_style_v1.json). Rules only — no source content.

## STRUCTURE

**Rules:**

1. **Open with a single declarative hook** — one punchy sentence establishing the race's defining narrative tension or irony, before any scene-setting.
2. **Funnel from the macro to the micro** — begin with championship/season-wide stakes, then zoom into the specific race incident that drove them, then into technical or procedural granularity.
3. **Use secondary storylines as discrete subsections**, each with its own bold subheading, following the main narrative; these run in descending order of drama, not chronology.
4. **Close the main narrative with a forward-looking question or stakes summary**, returning to the season frame opened in the first paragraph.
5. **Each subsection closes independently** with a consequence or irony beat, not a cliffhanger, before the next subheading begins.

**Skeleton Outline:**
```
[HOOK SENTENCE — defines the race's core irony or result]
[1–2 sentences framing season stakes]
[Scene reconstruction: leading into the key moment]
[Driver/team radio quote — direct, unedited]
[Technical explanation of the mechanism behind the moment]
[Consequence for standings / championship picture]
[Transitional observation — fairness, fortune, or irony]

## [SECONDARY STORYLINE HEADING]
[Scene-setting sentence]
[Procedural/regulatory explanation — how the system works]
[Evidence: data, official statements, rule text]
[Quote from affected party]
[Ruling or outcome]
[Closing irony or consequence beat]

## [TERTIARY STORYLINE HEADING — if needed]
[Same structure as above, compressed]

[FINAL PARAGRAPH — season-wide implication, open question, or tone-setting observation]
```

## EVIDENTIARY PRACTICE

**Rules:**

1. **Specific figures are deployed sparingly but precisely** — exact numerical values (gaps, speeds, percentages, fines) appear only where they clinch an argument or make a consequence tangible, never as decoration.
2. **Regulatory text is quoted or paraphrased directly**, with article numbers cited when the ruling is contested; this signals authority without over-explaining.
3. **Driver radio transcripts serve as primary evidence** for internal team dynamics; they are reproduced verbatim, including profanity and hesitation, to establish authenticity.
4. **Speculation is distinguished from fact by soft attribution** — phrases like "it is likely," "it could be," or "it seems" are used consistently for interpretive claims, never for verifiable outcomes.
5. **Technical mechanisms are explained causally** ("because X, Y happened") rather than merely described, grounding the claim in engineering or procedural logic before drawing conclusions.
6. **Team/manufacturer statements are included as a counter-voice**, not the final word — the writer's own interpretive gloss follows or precedes them.
7. **Championship arithmetic is stated plainly** at the point of maximum relevance, not summarised in an opening paragraph.

## REGISTER

**Rules:**

1. **Conversational-expert register** — writes as a knowledgeable insider explaining to an engaged non-specialist; never condescending, never jargon-dense without a follow-up explanation.
2. **Personality surfaces through analogies and asides** — cultural references and wry observations are embedded in the analytical flow, not bracketed off as jokes.
3. **The reader is addressed implicitly**, through shared assumption rather than direct "you" — the writer assumes the reader cares about the sport deeply and needs context, not persuasion.
4. **Technical vocabulary is used precisely then immediately contextualised** — a regulation article number or engineering term is always followed within a sentence or two by its plain-English consequence.
5. **Mild irreverence toward institutions** (governing bodies, team management) is permitted; drivers are treated with more consistent respect, even when criticised.
6. **Emotional register rises only at moments of genuine sporting drama** — superlatives and emphatic constructions are rationed so they land when deployed.

---

## PACING

**Rules:**

1. **Opening paragraphs are short and declarative** — rarely more than two sentences each, establishing rhythm before complexity builds.
2. **Density peaks at the technical explanation phase** — the longest, most clause-heavy sentences appear when a mechanism, regulation, or data point must be unpacked; surrounding sentences shorten to absorb the load.
3. **Driver quotes punctuate rather than replace analysis** — a quote is typically preceded by a contextualising sentence and followed by an interpretive sentence; quotes are never consecutive without analytical bridging.
4. **Each subsection resets the rhythm** — the subheading is followed by a short scene-setter before density builds again, mirroring the main article's structure in miniature.
5. **The piece dwells longest on the decisive race moment** — the pivotal on-track event receives the most paragraphs; ancillary storylines are proportionally compressed.
6. **Closing paragraphs shorten again**, returning to the punchy, declarative pace of the opening to signal resolution and forward momentum simultaneously.
