# Style dimensions — Jolyon Palmer — spoken analysis (tertiary)

> Distilled 2026-09-08 from 7 dev-split documents (see corpus/manifests/distill_style_v1.json). Rules only — no source content.

## STRUCTURE

1. **Open with a hook proposition or question**, immediately locating the focal event and its stakes in a single declarative sentence or short rhetorical frame before any description begins.
2. **Establish a layered agenda at the outset**: name the two or three sub-topics the piece will examine, then address each in turn without revisiting the order.
3. **Move from macro to micro**: begin with field-wide positioning (all cars, competitive context), then zoom to one driver's cockpit, then zoom again to a single data trace or body movement.
4. **Weave in a historical analogue mid-piece** as a standalone sub-beat—introduce it, extract one transferable lesson, then return to the present scenario without lingering.
5. **Use a question-and-answer beat** (either a sourced viewer question or a rhetorical self-posed question) to pivot between major sections rather than relying on transitional prose.
6. **Reserve verdict and broader implication for the final beat**: the closing paragraph reframes the micro-detail into a championship or team-level consequence, ending on a forward-looking or evaluative note rather than a summary.

**Skeleton outline:**
```
[HOOK PROPOSITION / FOCAL EVENT + STAKES]
  ↓
[AGENDA STATEMENT: "two/three things to look at…"]
  ↓
[BEAT 1 — field-level action, all relevant cars]
  ↓
[BEAT 2 — individual driver deep-dive, cockpit/telemetry layer]
  ↓
[HISTORICAL ANALOGUE sub-beat — introduce / extract lesson / return]
  ↓
[QUESTION-AND-ANSWER PIVOT — viewer Q or rhetorical Q]
  ↓
[BEAT 3 — data or second driver comparison]
  ↓
[VERDICT + CHAMPIONSHIP/TEAM IMPLICATION — forward-looking close]
```

## EVIDENTIARY PRACTICE

1. **Telemetry traces are the primary evidence tier**: throttle position, RPM, speed deltas, and energy-deployment graphs are named and walked through in real time, with the analyst narrating movement across the trace as it happens.
2. **Video replay is treated as a controllable instrument**: the analyst explicitly pauses, rewinds, and plays at reduced speed, narrating what each controlled replay reveals that live speed obscures.
3. **Numbers serve as anchors, not ornaments**: a specific figure (a gap in seconds or tenths, a gear number, a percentage) is deployed once to crystallise a claim, then the prose moves on—figures are never listed in clusters for their own sake.
4. **Causal chains are built step-by-step**: each mechanical or strategic cause is stated, its immediate effect named, then that effect is shown to produce a downstream consequence—three-link chains are typical, four-link chains appear at peak analytical density.
5. **Speculation is clearly bracketed** with first-person hedges ("maybe," "it seems to me," "I think," "I wonder if") and explicitly distinguished from what the data shows ("the data backs that up" vs. "I think"), never left ambiguous.
6. **Counter-evidence is pre-emptively raised and absorbed**: the analyst names the strongest alternative interpretation, concedes any partial validity, then explains why the primary reading still holds—steelmanning is built into the argumentative rhythm.
7. **Driver radio and team communications are used as corroborating witnesses**, not as primary evidence; they confirm rather than lead the argument.
8. **Simulation or counterfactual reasoning is signalled explicitly** ("what if," "had they," "according to our simulations") and always closed with a likelihood verdict rather than left open.

## REGISTER

1. **Conversational but technically credentialed**: vocabulary alternates freely between informal colloquial phrases and precise engineering terminology within the same sentence, never sounding either dumbed-down or academic.
2. **First-person experience is deployed sparingly as a credibility marker**: brief "I've been in that position" or "from my own experience" asides ground abstract claims in embodied expertise without becoming anecdotal.
3. **The reader/viewer is addressed as a curious co-analyst**, not a passive audience—the analyst thinks aloud, flags what he found surprising, and invites the reader to notice specific details as they emerge.
4. **Evaluative language is direct and unhedged when a clear verdict is available**: "great," "poor," "brilliant," "harsh," "ridiculous" appear without qualification when the evidence is deemed conclusive, signalling confident expert judgment.
5. **Humour and colour are used as pacing devices**, not personality performance: a vivid informal phrase or mild joke appears at the end of a dense technical passage to release tension, then the piece returns immediately to analysis.
6. **Technical terms are introduced in context without definition**: jargon (energy harvesting, MGU-K, anti-stall, undercut, dirty air) is used as though the audience is already familiar, elevating register and respecting reader competence.
7. **Rivalry and psychological motivation are treated as legitimate analytical categories** alongside mechanical data—driver decision-making is narrated in terms of risk/reward calculation, championship arithmetic, and ego, all given equal analytical weight to lap times.

## PACING

1. **Sentences expand and contract within a single paragraph**: a long, clause-heavy sentence walking through a sequence of events is immediately followed by a short, punchy verdict sentence of five words or fewer, creating a rhythmic heartbeat throughout.
2. **Each analytical beat occupies roughly one to three paragraphs** before the piece moves on; no single point is dwelt upon long enough to become exhaustive, and no point is dispatched so quickly as to feel asserted rather than argued.
3. **Density peaks at the telemetry/data layer**: the longest sentences and most embedded sub-clauses appear when narrating trace data; this is the slowest, most granular passage of any given section, and it is followed by a deliberate release into shorter sentences.
4. **Transition between beats is marked by a brief summary clause** ("so what we've seen here is…") followed immediately by a new agenda marker ("let's now look at…"), keeping forward momentum without abrupt jumps.
5. **Driver-quote inserts function as caesuras**: verbatim audio or transcribed speech appears at a natural pause point, always following a completed analytical argument, and the analysis resumes after the quote without back-referencing it directly.
6. **The historical analogue sub-beat is paced faster than the primary analysis**: it is introduced, illustrated with one or two beats, and closed within roughly the same space as a single primary beat, preventing the piece from feeling diverted.
7. **The final verdict paragraph is paced the slowest**: sentences lengthen, conditional constructions appear ("had X occurred, Y would have followed"), and the piece decelerates deliberately to signal conclusion before the last sentence, which is typically short and declarative.
